## 2. U28, answered: the objections are real, and they are semantic

The question was fair. "Linked timeouts are rejected" read as a verdict on the idiom, and that framing was too broad.
The idiom itself is sound — guard one SQE, let the kernel cancel the pair, no explicit cancel needed — and zigring
exposes it: `pushTimeoutForOp` (zigring `src/iouring.zig:1428`), and its own test uses it for the textbook case, an
accept that must not hang (`iouringtest.zig:959-1001`). The rejection stands for a narrower, checkable reason:

**A `LINK_TIMEOUT` guards exactly one SQE. None of the five deadlines the design pins is the completion of one SQE.**

Each against the mechanism:

1. **The greet-read deadline guards a phase (E2).** The design reads "exactly 5 bytes with a deadline" (§3.2) and
   re-arms on partial reads. A link timeout on each re-armed recv restarts its clock with every short completion: a peer
   drip-feeding one byte every 4.9 s holds a handshake entry open indefinitely — the slow-loris shape the deadline
   exists to close. The design's deadline is absolute from first arm to fifth byte; only a timer that outlives the
   recv's SQEs expresses it: one `pushTicker` (ticks = 1) armed at the accept, `pushCancel`ed when the fifth byte lands.
2. **The dialer's reply deadline is the same shape (G1).** "Reads exactly seven bytes with a deadline and re-arms
   partial reads" (§3.5, the doc's wording): one absolute deadline across a re-arming read sequence, not per-fragment
   clocks that a slow-drip peer resets.
3. **The predicate timeout times an ack, not a completion (H1).** The pulse's send CQE proves only that the kernel
   accepted the buffer (§4.8's own argument, letter 1's `U10`). What must be bounded is the arrival of a **later inbound
   frame** — the `pulse_ack` — on the standing receive arm, uncorrelated with the pulse by design ("an acknowledgement
   that arrives while a probe is outstanding satisfies every outstanding probe", §4.8). A link timeout bound to the
   pulse's send SQE fires only if the _send_ stalls, which a healthy send does not; it cannot wait for an event that
   happens on another arm after the guarded SQE is long complete. §3.4 already pins the right shape: a TaskCtx timeout
   carrying `{ host_idx, epoch }`, satisfied by the ack flag or left to fire.
4. **The keepalive and the retry have no guarded SQE at all (I1, G2).** The keepalive is a repeating producer
   (`pushTicker`, ticks = 0, the `armTicker` / `ShutdownTick` precedent, `src/index.zig:665`, `src/front.zig:221`); the
   dial retry is a pure re-armed delay carrying the current backoff. A `LINK_TIMEOUT` binds to a preceding SQE; there is
   none to bind to.
5. **The accounting and the friction, both verified in this tree.** Every guarded arm costs two completions — one side
   of the pair always arrives `-ECANCELED` (zigring's test consumes the pair, `iouringtest.zig:976-998`) — so each guard
   needs a second TaskCtx slot whose only job is absorbing an expected cancel, and the guarded op's handler must split
   "canceled by my own guard" from a real failure, on the plan's two hottest completion paths. And the SDK shape is
   batch-only: the guard is reliable only when it travels in the same `batchedSQ` batch as the guarded op and they
   commit together — "a bare ring push may lose the association and the kernel then rejects the SQE with `-EINVAL`"
   (zigring `src/iouring.zig:1415-1427`, mirrored for the batch API at `:1828-1831`); on `error.RingFull` the pair
   unwinds and re-forms. Nothing in the tree uses the batch API today (`rg` finds no `batchedSQ`, no `pushTimeoutForOp`
   in `src/`). Against that, a taskIdx-named one-shot ticker plus a plain `pushCancel` is the house idiom, proven three
   times over: the init deadline (`src/main.zig:260-267`), the snapshot arm/cancel pair (`src/index.zig:665-692`), the
   front's shutdown cancel (`src/front.zig:187-224`).

One further constraint closes the nearest alternative: the "pure" timeout op is not available as a fallback.
`pushTimeout` with a zero `len` is rejected by this kernel — `src/main.zig:260-262` records the trap, and the tree's
only `pushTimeout` call is a test that fills the SQ and never asserts on those CQEs (`src/snapshot.zig:1523-1528`). So a
one-shot `pushTicker` (ticks = 1) is the SDK's only usable one-shot timer, and it is the mechanism on every deadline
above. That is why `waitForInitialized` already uses it (`src/main.zig:267`).

Letter 5's three supporting claims all check out — nothing in the tree uses linked timeouts; the SDK constrains them to
a shared batch; the zero-length `pushTimeout` trap is real. What was missing was the reason, and the reason is the
semantic mismatch of section 2, not any defect of the io_uring scheme.

## 3. The concession: one edge where the linked timeout is the right tool

The dialer's `pushConnectIp4` (G1) is the plan's one true SQE-shaped bound: a single submit that can hang until the TCP
stack gives up, and the design pins no connect deadline today — §3.5 bounds the reply read, §3.6's "greeting timeout" is
that reply deadline, and the SYN retransmit budget is the only guard on the dial itself. If you want the connect inside
the `handshake_timeout` budget, the linked timeout is the natural mechanism: the guard rides in the same `batchedSQ`
batch as the connect SQE, and the plan would use `pushTimeoutForOp` for this one arm only — the tree's first batch-API
use, with the pair-unwind rule noted in §3.0's standing rules. It is optional: the design as promoted leaves the connect
to the TCP stack, and on the §K loopback harness the connect completes immediately either way. Proposed as part (b) of
U28 below — adopt or decline.
