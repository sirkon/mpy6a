## 2. The one correction: the connect's guard is a ticker, not a linked timeout

Letter 6 section 3 proposed `pushTimeoutForOp` riding in the same `batchedSQ` batch as the connect SQE. Compiling the
plan means verifying every symbol it names (the plan's own rule 2), and the SDK does not have that shape:

- `BatchSQ` (zigring `src/iouring.zig:1638-1887`) exposes no connect push. Its whole surface is `pushWrite`,
  `pushWriteOffset`, `pushReadOffset`, `pushSend`, `pushSendZC`, `pushAccept`, `pushTicker`, `pushTimeoutForOp`,
  `pushCancel` and `commit`. The connect exists only as `Ring.pushConnectIp4` / `pushConnectIp6` (`iouring.zig:1174`,
  `:1222`), and the `pushConnect` behind them is not public.
- Two bare ring pushes cannot substitute. `commitOp` (`iouring.zig:421-451`) publishes the SQ tail after each push and wakes the
  SQPOLL thread as soon as the SQ was empty, so the poller can consume the connect alone; the `IOSQE_IO_LINK` chain is
  cut at that submission boundary and the guard is then rejected with `-EINVAL` — zigring's own doc names the trap ("a
  bare ring push may lose the association and the kernel then rejects the SQE with `-EINVAL`", `iouring.zig:1415-1427`). On the
  first dial the index ring is quiet, the SQ is empty, and the wakeup fires between the two pushes: the guard would be
  lost almost every time, leaving the connect to the SYN retransmit budget — exactly the window part (b) closes.

So the compiled plan bounds the connect the way it bounds every other deadline: a one-shot `pushTicker` (ticks = 1) in a
`connect_deadline { host_idx }` slot armed with the dial and `pushCancel`ed on the connect CQE; on its tick the connect
itself is `pushCancel`ed, and the canceled dial is a failed connect under §3.6 — warn, close, retry. This is the house
idiom, proven by the init deadline (`src/main.zig:260-267`) and the snapshot arm/cancel pair, and it needs no batch API
and no `-EINVAL` retry path. Letter 6's semantic claim stands — the connect is the plan's one SQE-shaped bound — but
with this SDK the linked timeout cannot express even that one arm reliably. If you want the racy bare-pair variant anyway
(the connect pushed with `TaskFlags.expectNext()`, the guard pushed behind it, a guard CQE of `-EINVAL` handled by
cancelling the connect and re-forming the pair), say so in a `# Critical` item; the thread is otherwise closed by this
compilation.
